01

IngestAI

Document intelligence, semantic retrieval & agent platform

Ollon · 2026

A seven-service, three-environment GCP platform that reads a company's paperwork, extracts it under evaluation, puts a human on the uncertain calls, and then lets agents answer questions across the whole corpus from grounded sources.

I was the primary technical owner and sole repository CODEOWNER, from the first commit to production traffic in 28 weeks across 429 merged pull requests.

The intake half is multi-tenant ingestion of PDF, XLSX, and Google Sheets through Gemini extraction, confidence scoring, human review, and system-of-record delivery. Fourteen of fourteen validated production pushes landed with zero orphaned records, which is the number I care about most: the pipeline either delivers a complete job or it delivers nothing.

The semantic layer is mine end to end. Chunking and embedding workers cover 26,000 historical documents at 99.98% coverage and feed pgvector retrieval, so the agents answer from grounded source passages rather than from the extraction database alone.

On top of that sits a governed MCP server and agent architecture: 40 tools over 21 BI views, with tenant and principal isolation, audited queries, answers grounded in a cited source, and conversational surfaces deployed on Gemini Enterprise.

Extraction quality was treated as an engineering problem rather than a prompt problem. A golden-data evaluation harness wired into CI as a release gate moved quantity recall from 37% to 99%, and reached 96.3% precision at 91% coverage on a taxonomy mapping field that had resisted every heuristic before it.

The unglamorous half was making it a good place to work. CI runtime came down 52%, and the local unit test loop went from 10.5 minutes to 15.6 seconds.

Building now

Two products I am building on my own time.

Both start from the same observation: multimodal models just changed what is possible, and most of the interesting work is in the unglamorous half, the evaluation, the confidence handling, and the moment a human has to be asked.

KitchenOS

Stop wasting the food you already bought.

v0 on device

An operating system keeps state so the programs do not have to. KitchenOS keeps the state of a kitchen: what is there, what is dying, and what is possible tonight.

Every app that attacked food waste asked people to become inventory clerks, and every one of them died of that ask. Multimodal models changed the physics. A camera glance can read a fridge now, and a spoken sentence can update it. The bet is that a fuzzy, confidence-scored, staleness-tolerant picture of a fridge is sustainable where exact bookkeeping never was.

Native SwiftUI with a WidgetKit face on the Home and Lock Screen, on-device SwiftData, and three narrow agent surfaces: vision inventory inference, voice delta parsing, and constrained recipe generation. The design constitution is its own document, and the simulator is the source of truth for what the app is.

Manifest

Meet your data where it lives, and make it make sense.

Pre-v0, first slice live

Manifest turns the documents a company already receives, invoices and receipts and POs and job paperwork, into organized files and normalized records in that company's own vocabulary: their jobs, their vendors, their cost types.

The bet is that it is configurable without developers. Taxonomies do not transfer between companies, and that is the point. An admin brings their own vocabulary and the engine stays constant. Every human correction becomes remembered mapping, which is the part a generic PDF-to-JSON tool cannot copy.

The first vertical slice runs intake, extraction, and normalization across three deliberately different company shapes at once, because forcing three taxonomies through one engine from day one is the only honest proof of the one-engine bet. Verifying a document in the review surface mints an eval expectation, so the review queue doubles as the accuracy corpus.

Approach

How I keep AI systems honest.

Shipping a model into production is mostly a question of what you refuse to claim without proof. These are the lines I hold, taken from the constitutions I write for my own projects.

01

Evidence over vibes

A trust claim ships with the measured run behind it. If accuracy cannot be stated as a number from a real eval, it does not go in the deck.

02

Demonstrated, not merged

A behavior exists when someone has watched it work on the real device or the real environment. Merged code is a claim, not a result.

03

Plain code by default

Agents earn their place against the boring alternative. Where a function will do, a function does it, and the model handles the part that genuinely needs judgment.

04

Never guess silently

Below the confidence bar, the system asks. One wrong silent record costs more trust than ten honest questions, and every automatic decision carries its evidence and a one-click correction.
